### What a buyer needs from your page

A stranger lands on your page with three questions: what is this, is it for me, and what do I get when I pay? If the page answers those in the first screen, they keep reading.

Guides written by sellers say much the same thing. One lists the basics as what the product is, what problem it solves, what is inside and what the buyer receives. Another says to write each feature and then add what it means for the buyer. That is a good rule, and we use it below.

The AI cannot do any of this from a product name. It needs the facts, and it needs rules about what it may not do.

### The weak prompt and what it gave

Our example product is made up so you can follow along. It is a four-page PDF checklist for pet sitters: a handover sheet the owner fills in before the first visit. Here is the prompt most people would type.

```
Write a Gumroad product description for my pet sitter checklist.
```

Output (generated with Claude Sonnet 5.5, 2026-11-07), trimmed to the best lines:

```
Introducing the Ultimate Pet Sitter Checklist!

Whether you're a seasoned pet sitter or just starting out, this comprehensive guide will help you provide the best care for your furry friends. Packed with valuable tips, it makes pet sitting stress-free and organized.

What's included:
- Detailed checklists
- Helpful tips
- Easy-to-use format

Get yours today and take your pet sitting to the next level!
```

Look at “detailed checklists” and “helpful tips”. They say nothing. A buyer cannot tell what is on page two, whether it is for dog owners or cat owners, or whether it is something they fill in or print. Also “seasoned or just starting out” is for everyone, which means it is for no one.

### Fix 1: Give it the facts and the buyer

Write what is true. Number of pages, file type, what each section is for, who it helps. Also write who it is not for. That last line is the one most sellers skip, and it is the one that makes a reader trust the page.

Next, name the buyer in one sentence. “Pet sitters who take home visits and need the owner to hand over the details” is a buyer. “Pet lovers” is a crowd.

### Fix 2: Tell it what is banned

AI product pages share a vocabulary. Ultimate, comprehensive, game-changing, take it to the next level. You know the list because you have rolled your eyes at it. Put those words in a ban list, and tell the AI to name what each part does instead.

Ask for the feature and its effect on the buyer in one line each. “Medication times in one table, so you never scroll through texts to find them” is a feature with a benefit. “Detailed checklists” is neither.

### Fix 3: Add the TODO rule

Models fill gaps. If you do not give a price, a refund policy or a file size, the AI will write one that sounds right. The remedy is a single sentence: if a fact is missing, write TODO. Then you see the gap and fill it yourself, instead of publishing an invented promise.

Here is the full prompt.

```
Write the description for my Gumroad product page.

Product facts (use only these):
- A 4-page PDF checklist for pet sitters who visit or stay in the owner's home.
- The owner fills it in before the first visit; the sitter keeps a copy.
- Sections: feeding and medication times in one table; vet name, phone number and the most the owner will approve without calling; where things are (leash, spare key, cleaning supplies); quirks (what scares the dog, where the cat hides); a visit log to tick off.
- Works printed or filled in on a phone.
- Not for boarding kennels, which have their own intake forms.
- Price: not decided.

Rules:
- Start with one sentence saying who it is for and what changes for them.
- Then a bullet list: each bullet is the feature, then what it means for the sitter.
- Then Who it is not for and After you buy (what they download and what to do first).
- No exclamation marks. Do not use: ultimate, comprehensive, game-changing, stress-free, next level.
- Do not invent facts, numbers or promises. If something is missing, write TODO.
- Under 170 words.
```

Output (generated with Claude Sonnet 5.5, 2026-11-07), trimmed to the best lines:

```
A 4-page handover checklist for pet sitters, so the owner's instructions are on paper before the first visit and not buried in texts.

- Feeding and medication times in one table, so you never scroll back through messages to find the dose.
- Vet name, phone number and a spending limit, so you know what you can approve without calling the owner.
- A where-things-are list (leash, spare key, cleaning supplies), so you do not search the house.
- A quirks section, so you know what scares the dog and where the cat hides.
- A visit log you tick off, so the owner can see each visit happened.

Works printed or filled in on a phone.

Who it is not for: boarding kennels, which have their own intake forms.

After you buy: download the PDF, send it to the owner before your first visit, and keep a copy.

Price: TODO
```

Same product, same model. Every line now comes from a fact you gave. The price shows up as TODO, which is exactly what you wanted: a gap you can see.

### Do the price math before you fill in TODO

Price is the TODO you must fill in yourself, and the AI should not guess it. What it can do is help you see what a sale leaves you.

The pricing page of the platform lists 10% + 50 cents per transaction for sales through your profile or direct links, and 30% when new customers find you through its Discover marketplace.

So at 9 dollars through your own link, the fee is 90 cents plus 50 cents, which is 1.40 dollars. You keep 7.60. At 5 dollars the fee is 1.00, so one dollar in five goes to the platform. Through Discover at 9 dollars, the fee is 2.70 and you keep 6.30.

Those are the platform fees only. Work them out once, then pick a price you are happy with at the lower figure.

If you want a second opinion on the number, give the AI your facts and the competing prices you saw on the results page. Ask it to explain the trade-off. Do not ask it what will sell best.

### Fix 4: Ask for the objection line

Before you publish, run one more pass. Paste the page and your facts back in and ask: “What would a careful buyer worry about after reading this, and which of my facts answers it?”

For the pet sitter page it might say the buyer worries about whether the file works on a phone. The page already says so, and you can move that line higher.

If it finds a worry your facts do not answer, you have found a gap in the page or in the product. Fix it before the first customer finds it for you.

### Before you publish

Do not paste fake reviews or praise into the page. A made-up quote does more harm than an empty space, and fake reviews break the rules on most platforms. If you have real feedback, add it later, word for word.

Do not let the AI write what is inside the file from memory. Give it a list from the file itself. And do not publish before you read every line aloud. If a sentence sounds like an ad, cut it.
