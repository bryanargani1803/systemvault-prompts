### Why the first list is useless

An AI model has no view of Etsy or Gumroad when you chat with it. It cannot see what sells today, who the sellers are or what people type into the search box. So when you ask for ideas, it gives you the average of everything it has read about side businesses.

That is the weak prompt, and you can see the result below. We ran it in Claude Sonnet 5.5 on November 7, 2026, and kept the best lines.

```
Give me digital product ideas I can sell online.
```

Output (generated with Claude Sonnet 5.5, 2026-11-07), trimmed:

```
Here are some digital product ideas you can sell online:

1. Ebooks on a topic you know well
2. Online courses
3. Printable planners and journals
4. Templates (resumes, social media, spreadsheets)
5. Meal plans and workout programs

Tip: Pick a niche you are passionate about and research your audience.
```

Read the last line. “Research your audience” is the whole job, and the answer hands it back to you. Nothing in this list belongs to you, because you never said who you are.

### Step 1: Tell it what is true about you

The strong prompt starts with facts, and facts are cheap. You already know them.

What do you know how to do? Which tools do you use well? Who can you reach, even if it is three people? How many hours do you have?

Say what you do not want, too. If you will not record video, say so, and the course ideas disappear.

Here is a made-up person to follow along: a former café manager. She wrote the staff rota every week for six years, trained new hires and ran opening and closing. She uses Google Sheets and Canva. She has no audience yet.

### Step 2: Ask for ideas you can check, not ideas you must believe

This is the part most prompts miss. You do not ask the AI whether an idea will sell. It cannot know. You ask it to say what you should see on the results page if people already buy this kind of product, and what would make you drop the idea.

That turns a guess into a test you can run in ten minutes. Add one more rule: anything the model is guessing must be marked GUESS, so a guess cannot pass for a fact.

That small word has a big effect. Without it, the AI writes guesses and facts in the same confident voice, and you cannot tell them apart. With it, every claim arrives labelled, and the ones to check first are the ones marked GUESS.

```
I want to sell one small digital product on Gumroad or Etsy. Here is what is true about me.

What I know: I managed a small café for six years. I wrote the staff rota every week, trained new hires and ran opening and closing.
Tools I use well: Google Sheets and Canva.
Who I can reach: no audience yet, only a few café owners I know by name.
Time: about 10 hours a week for one month.
Not interested in: video courses.

Give me 5 product ideas that fit these facts. For each one write:
- Who buys it, in one sentence.
- The exact phrase that buyer would type into Etsy or Google.
- What I should see on the results page if people already buy this. Do not state sales numbers; you cannot see the market.
- The sign that means I should drop the idea.
Mark anything you are guessing with GUESS.
```

Output (generated with Claude Sonnet 5.5, 2026-11-07), trimmed to the first two ideas:

```
1. Weekly staff rota template (Google Sheets)
Who buys it: A small café or shop owner who still builds the rota on paper.
Search phrase: “staff schedule template google sheets”
Look for: Several listings for the same kind of template, from different sellers, with reviews. A price range you can read off the page.
Drop it if: Only one shop sells it, or every result is free.

2. Opening and closing checklist (printable PDF)
Who buys it: A new manager who has never been given a list.
Search phrase: “restaurant opening closing checklist”
Look for: PDF listings aimed at cafés, not only large restaurants.
Drop it if: Every result is a long generic bundle and none targets cafés. GUESS: that gap may exist.
```

Now compare it with the first list. Each idea has a buyer, a phrase to search and a way to fail. You can act on it today.

### Step 3: Spend ten minutes on the results page

Take the search phrase for idea one and type it into Etsy. Then type it into Google, and look at other stores too. You are not looking for a number. You are looking for four things.

First, are there several sellers, not one? Several sellers means people buy it. A single seller could mean a gap, or it could mean nobody wants it.

Second, do the listings have reviews? Reviews mean that someone paid.

Third, can you read a price range off the page? That tells you what buyers accept.

Fourth, who is each product for? If every result is for offices and none says café, you have found a way to be different.

Write your notes in plain words. Do not try to be exact. “Most have reviews, prices run from a few dollars to about twenty, none say café” is a perfectly good note.

Trust what you see on the listing pages more than what any headline promises. Listings show what people really pay for. Headlines only show what gets clicks.

### Step 4: Give the AI your notes and ask for a verdict

Now the AI can help again, because it has real information from you. Paste your notes and ask for one of three answers: go, change or drop. Make it give a reason in two sentences, so it cannot waffle.

Here are example notes we typed for this article. They are not real search results.

```
Here is what I saw when I searched “staff schedule template google sheets” on Etsy and Gumroad. Judge only from these notes.
- Some of the first results are for restaurants. The rest are for offices or nurses.
- Most have reviews. Prices run from a few dollars to about twenty.
- None mention cafés or tips.
- One bundle puts a rota inside a large pack of sheets.

Tell me: go, change, or drop? Give the reason in two sentences, then the one change that would make my listing different. If my notes are not enough to decide, say what to look at next.
```

Output (generated with Claude Sonnet 5.5, 2026-11-07), trimmed:

```
Verdict: change.

Reason: People clearly buy rota sheets, since most listings have reviews and a visible price range. But every result is built for restaurants, offices or nurses, so a café owner has to adapt it.

The change: Sell it as a “Café staff rota” with columns for opening and closing shifts and a tab for splitting tips. Put “café” in the title and the first image.
```

The verdict does not say the product will sell. It says that the idea has demand signals and a gap you can name. That is enough to start building, and it is more than the first list ever gave you.

Notice what the AI did not do. It did not invent a sales figure or promise you a result. It only read your notes and told you what they mean, which is the one job it can do well here.

### Step 5: Run it twice and compare

One idea tells you little. Two ideas tell you which one is stronger.

Take the second idea from the list, the opening and closing checklist, and do Steps 3 and 4 again. Keep your notes short so you can set them side by side. Then paste both sets into the AI and ask which gap is clearer.

A clear gap has three signs. People already buy the product, because there are reviews. The buyers are not served well, because nothing names them. And you can say in one sentence what yours does that the others do not.

If both ideas pass, pick the one you can build in the fewest hours. A small product that exists beats a perfect one that does not. If both fail, you have lost twenty minutes, not a month.

This is also why the drop rule matters. When you feel attached to an idea, you will find reasons to keep it. A rule written before you looked at the results is much harder to argue with.

Keep a note of each check, with the date. In three months you will want to know why you chose one idea and dropped another.

### The mistakes that waste your month

The first mistake is asking the AI to predict sales. It will answer with confidence and be guessing. Ask for what to check instead.

The second mistake is leaving your facts out. Without them you get the average list again. The third is skipping the drop rule. If you do not decide in advance what failure looks like, you will talk yourself into every idea.

The fourth is validating one idea only. Run the check on two or three, and build the one with the clearest gap. Each check takes about ten minutes.
